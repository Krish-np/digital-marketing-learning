# Practical Activities

## Activity 1 — Observe a Real Business

Pick a business you know: a local shop, a restaurant, an online store — whatever's easiest to look up.

Then dig around a bit and answer:

- Do they have a website?
- Are they active on social media?
- Do they show up in Google search?
- What kind of content do they post?
- Where could they realistically improve?

Write a short summary of what you found.

---

## Activity 2 — Analyze a Website

Visit any business website you like and take a proper look at it.

Ask yourself:

- Is it easy to find your way around?
- Can you spot the contact details quickly?
- Are there clear calls to action?
- Does it work well on a phone?
- Does it load fast, or are you left waiting?

Note down three things it does well and three things you'd change.

# Mini Project 2

## Map a Customer Journey

Think about something you bought online recently, and trace the path you took to get there.

Here's an example:

```text
Google search
      ↓
Website visit
      ↓
Read product details
      ↓
Compare prices
      ↓
Purchase
      ↓
Confirmation email
      ↓
Product delivery
      ↓
Review
```

Now reflect:

- What actually tipped you into buying?
- Where did the experience fall short?
- Would you buy from them again? Why or why not?

# Discussion Questions

There's no marking scheme here and no "correct" column to check yourself against. Just sit with these for a minute before you answer.

- Why do some businesses take off online while others post consistently for a year and hear crickets?
- Could a business actually survive today without a website? Where would that fall apart?
- Does every business genuinely need social media, or have we just decided that it does?
- If you were starting something tomorrow and could only use _one_ digital marketing channel, which would you pick? And be honest about why.

# Challenge

Pick a business — a real one you know, one you've worked at, or one you've been quietly daydreaming about starting. Then write a one-page digital marketing improvement plan for it.

Cover:

- **Who they're actually trying to reach** — be specific, "everyone" isn't an audience
- **Which channels you'd recommend** — and just as importantly, which ones you'd skip
- **A handful of content ideas** — things they could realistically make
- **SEO suggestions** — start with the basics before anything clever
- **A social media approach** — what they'd post, where, and how often without burning out
- **How you'd know it's working** — pick a few numbers that genuinely tell you something

Keep it grounded. The point isn't to write a strategy deck for a global brand with a media budget. It's to figure out what _this_ business, with the hours and money it actually has, could start doing on Monday morning.
